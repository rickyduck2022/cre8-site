# Music licensing explained: a practical UK guide for cre8

**Prepared:** 11 November 2026  
**Audience:** founders, creators and product contributors; no legal background assumed  
**Status:** educational planning guide, not legal advice or a ready-to-sign licence  
**PDF:** [Download this guide](./music-licensing-explained-uk.pdf).  
**Next step:** use the [UK legal checklist](./uk-licensing-and-payments.md) with a music solicitor.

## The short version

A licence is **permission to do particular things with someone else's work**.
It is not necessarily ownership, and one permission does not automatically
include every other use.

For cre8, keep three separate questions:

1. **Can this creator authorize cre8 to host and deliver the music?**
2. **What can this listener or buyer do with the music after receiving it?**
3. **Does cre8 have any additional publishing/collective licensing obligations?**

An account, payment, A-List badge or working download button answers none of
these by itself.

## 1. One track can have several owners

Think of a song as both a recipe and a particular recording of that recipe.

| Right | Plain-English meaning | Who might control it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition/publishing | The underlying music and lyrics: the "recipe" | Writers, co-writers, publishers; licensing bodies may administer particular rights |
| Master/sound recording | The particular recorded performance | Producer, artist, label or another recording owner |
| Performers' rights | Separate rights associated with recorded performances | Singers, musicians and other performers, subject to their agreements |
| Other incorporated material | Samples, artwork, photographs or other borrowed work | The relevant third-party owners |

**Example:** a producer records a track with a vocalist, uses a sample and signs
the master to a label. Uploading the producer's own WAV is not enough evidence:
the label, composition owners, sample owners and performer arrangements may all
matter. Permission must cover the intended uses, not just possession of the file.

UK copyright generally arises automatically. Buying a copy or receiving a file
does not transfer its copyright. An assignment transfers ownership; a licence
permits uses while ownership can remain with the creator [1].

## 2. What cre8 needs permission to do

The creator-to-platform licence should cover the real service, in understandable
terms reviewed by a solicitor:

| cre8 activity | Permission to address |
| --- | --- |
| Accept an upload and keep backups | Store and reproduce the work for the service |
| Transcode audio or generate previews | Make the relevant processed copies |
| Let members select and stream tracks | Make music available/communicate it under the agreed conditions |
| Offer a file or pack ZIP | Make and distribute permitted downloadable copies |
| Show artwork and release descriptions | Display the relevant image/text material |
| Use a host, CDN or processing provider | Permit necessary service-provider handling, appropriately limited |
| Promote a release in an advert | Obtain the separate promotional uses required; do not assume normal hosting covers them |

State the territory, duration, permitted audiences and what happens on withdrawal.
A non-exclusive, purpose-limited platform licence is the suggested starting
point: cre8 should not need ownership of an artist's catalogue just to host it.
This is a drafting objective, not contractual wording.

**There are two permission checks:** the copyright/licence must allow the use,
and cre8's audience/grant rules must allow this particular member to access it.
A valid copyright licence does not mean every member is entitled to every file.

## 3. What the listener or buyer is allowed to do

### A Dub Pack

A licence might permit personal listening and an authorized download.
That does not automatically permit sampling, uploading elsewhere, public
performance, broadcasting, commercial release or resale.

If a creator wants to permit DJ use, define what that means. A permission to
download a dub is not a universal licence for every venue, livestream or DJ mix.
The DJ, venue, broadcaster or platform may have separate rights obligations.

### A Sample Pack

A buyer usually wants permission to incorporate sounds into new productions.
The licence must explain whether commercial releases are allowed, whether
attribution is needed, and what restrictions remain.

One possible product policy is:

> Permit incorporation into new musical productions, including commercial
> releases, but prohibit redistribution or resale of the standalone samples.

That is an **illustration to discuss with a solicitor**, not a complete licence
or a permission automatically granted by cre8 today.

Also decide: permitted users/seats, territory/duration, collaboration, transfer,
Content ID registration, attribution, AI-training use and any unusual limits.
Avoid vague claims such as "full rights included".

**Important:** a sample-library licence allowing use in a finished track often
does not allow extracting and reselling the same sounds in another Sample Pack.
Read the source licence before accepting or selling that pack.

## 4. What common licence words actually mean

| Term | Meaning to check, not assume |
| --- | --- |
| Royalty-free | Specified uses do not incur the additional royalties described by that licence; not copyright-free or universally cleared |
| Non-exclusive | Other people can receive similar permissions |
| Exclusive | Particular rights are reserved to someone; identify exactly which rights, territory and period, and check existing grants |
| Commercial use | Some money-making uses are allowed; the definition and exclusions still matter |
| Attribution | Credit must be given in the agreed way |
| Perpetual | A duration term; check termination conditions and whether the grant can validly cover that period |
| Worldwide | Geographic scope, not a guarantee that every necessary right is owned |
| Synchronization | Use of music with visual content, such as a video; do not assume an audio-only licence covers it |
| Mechanical rights | Reproduction rights in the musical work, rather than ownership of the master |

Free content can still have restrictive terms. Paid content can still have
restrictions. A licence cannot validly grant rights the licensor does not control.
Do not silently relicense an entire creator catalogue under one site-wide label.

## 5. Where PRS, MCPS and PPL fit

**PRS for Music / MCPS:** relevant composition/publishing and reproduction
permissions may be administered collectively. Explain cre8's complete service
to PRS and obtain a written assessment of repertoire, territories, uses,
reporting and fees. A direct artist agreement may not cover rights already
assigned to publishers or administered under other agreements [2].

**PPL:** handles particular recorded-music licensing activities, but it does not
offer a blanket solution for everything cre8 does. Its online guidance directs
user-upload services and services offering on-demand tracks/downloads to the
recording rightsholders for direct licences [3].

**TheMusicLicence:** do not confuse a licence for playing music at a business
premises with permission to run an on-demand upload/download platform.

**Practical consequence:** do not buy a small clip or radio licence and assume it
covers full-track streaming, selectable playlists, downloads and Sample Pack
resale. Get the correct scope confirmed. A 30-second clip is not automatically
exempt; PPL's clip product has specific conditions.

No licence fee estimate is given here: written quotes and rights-clearance costs
must be added to the [business plan](./business-plan-uk.md). Hosting costs do not
include music royalties.

## 6. Five everyday examples

| Situation | What to check/do |
| --- | --- |
| A creator uploads a wholly original track they wrote and recorded alone | Verify their ownership/control, any society/publisher/label commitments and the platform grant; "original" does not finish the enquiry |
| A producer uploads an unofficial remix of a well-known release | Require appropriate master/composition and adaptation clearances; do not treat credit or free distribution as permission |
| A producer uses a purchased royalty-free drum loop in a finished track | Check the library permits that release and platform delivery; retain the licence evidence |
| The same producer sells that loop unchanged in a Sample Pack | Do not assume it is allowed; redistribution of isolated sounds may be prohibited |
| An approved member shares a private pack with a friend | Both the licence and the platform's explicit recipient access must allow it; A-List approval does not grant onward-sharing rights |

These are screening examples, not definitive legal rulings on individual works.
If rights are uncertain, pause the affected upload/sale and investigate.

## 7. The evidence to keep

For each release/pack, record:

- Creator identity and relevant owner/licensor contacts.
- Writer/master/contributor details and any restrictions.
- Source sample licences, collaborator permissions and direct/collective grants
  relevant to the intended service; keep evidence, not just a checked box.
- The permitted uses, territories, audiences and duration.
- Licence version, acceptance timestamp and the agreed files/version.
- For paid packs, a purchase receipt and durable record of the accepted buyer
  licence and content manifest; do not depend on today's editable pack.

Retain only necessary personal information, restrict access to evidence and use
a documented legal/tax/privacy retention policy. Sensitive agreements should not
be public or committed to source control.

**Editing matters:** cre8 lets owners change pack details, type and membership.
Changing an existing pack must not silently rewrite a buyer's past purchase or
licence. Define whether updates are included and how removals, rights disputes,
seller departure and valid purchased access are handled.

## 8. What the product should make clear

Suggested future UX, **not an implementation claim**:

1. Upload: ask who owns/controls the rights, flag third-party samples/remixes and
   explain the specific permission being granted to cre8.
2. Publish: show audience and licence separately; check missing rights information.
3. Pack page: display a concise licence summary with the full version available
   before download or purchase.
4. Checkout: identify seller/licensor and pack version, capture required contract
   choices and send the receipt/licence. Follow the separate consumer-law rules
   in the [UK checklist](./uk-licensing-and-payments.md).
5. Account/library: preserve evidence of valid purchases independently of Pro
   status and mutable owner grants.
6. Reporting: provide a visible rights-complaint route, review/escalation process
   and access/cache removal where necessary.

Current audience controls and a free-form licence field are helpful foundations,
not proof that this rights/commerce workflow already exists.

## 9. Questions to take to a music solicitor and PRS

- Which rights must cre8 obtain for uploads, on-demand streams, previews,
  downloads, ZIPs and private sharing? Which can creators directly grant?
- What changes when a creator has a label, publisher, collecting-society
  membership, co-writers or sampled material?
- Does any proposed collective licence cover this specific service, repertoire,
  territories and volume? What reporting/minimum fees/royalties apply?
- What should our creator agreement and non-exclusive Sample Pack buyer licence
  say about commercial use, Content ID, updates and termination?
- Which rights remain after a creator removes a pack or a buyer receives a refund?
  Do not assume an already-downloaded file can technically be recalled.
- What claims/removal/appeal procedures and record retention are appropriate
  for cre8's actual role? What should happen to disputed purchased content?

## Bottom line

**Permission to host, permission to access and permission to reuse are different.**
Keep them separate in contracts, the UI and the database. Start with a curated,
documented-rights catalogue and get the service-specific licensing position
confirmed before treating either free distribution or paid packs as cleared.
