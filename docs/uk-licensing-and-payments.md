# cre8: UK licensing, payments and launch checklist

**Prepared:** 11 November 2026  
**Scope:** UK-based music community, streaming/downloads, subscriptions and a possible Sample Pack marketplace.  
**Status:** practical issue-spotting guide, not legal advice or a substitute for a UK solicitor/accountant.  
**PDF edition:** [Download PDF](./uk-licensing-and-payments.pdf).  
**Related:** [monetization strategy](./monetization-strategy.md), [business plan](./business-plan-uk.md).

## 1. Decisions to make before accepting payments

cre8 combines user uploads, listening, downloads, messaging and controlled
distribution. It is not simply a software subscription or a static download shop.
Different rights and legal duties can apply to each activity, even when free.

| Decision | Suggested low-complexity starting position | Evidence/owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business identity | Choose sole trader or limited company with advice; clearly identify the contracting trader | Founder + accountant: entity, bank/accounting records and customer disclosures |
| Customer/seller scope | UK-first pilot; sellers and paying pilot creators 18+ | Founder: geographic/age eligibility rules and proportionate enforcement |
| First paid product | cre8's own monthly Creator Pro workflow subscription | Product + solicitor: accurate benefit and cancellation terms |
| Music catalogue | Curated uploads with documented permissions, not assumed rights | Rights lead + music solicitor: rights records and licence agreements |
| Marketplace | Defer until seller, tax, payment and entitlement model is approved | Solicitor + accountant + provider: written money-flow diagram |
| Territories | Match streaming, downloads and sample-use rights to agreed territories | Rights lead: restrictions and delivery controls, including shared links |

Naming a product "B2B" does not automatically remove consumer protections:
whether a DJ/producer is acting as a consumer or a business depends on the facts.
An 18+ term also does not by itself settle child-access duties.

## 2. Music rights: what needs permission?

### Separate the layers

| Layer | Example | Questions for cre8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition/publishing | Melody, lyrics, underlying song | Who controls communication/making-available and relevant reproduction rights? Are publishers/PRS/MCPS involved? |
| Sound recording/master | The uploaded recording | Can the uploader license hosting, streaming, previews, downloads and processing? Does a label control it? |
| Performers/contributors | Vocalist, session player, collaborators | Are required performer permissions, releases and collaborator agreements in place? |
| Samples/remixes/artwork | Borrowed recording or composition, cover image | Is third-party use cleared for these activities and territories, including onward sample licensing? |

Copyright arises automatically; ownership does not follow merely from possessing
a WAV file or paying for a subscription [L1]. A producer can control a master
without controlling all publishing or incorporated samples.

### Permissions the service must actually have

Ask a music solicitor to draft an uploader agreement covering the specific
operations: upload/storage, backup, transcoding, preview generation, display of
artwork/metadata, streaming, authorized downloads, pack ZIP creation and required
service-provider sublicensing. Scope it by audience, territory, duration and
termination; do not take unrelated ownership of creators' music.

- A-List status is access approval, not a copyright licence.
- A private pack or message does not automatically remove copyright duties.
- Free tracks still require relevant permissions.
- There is no blanket "under 30 seconds" or "small sample" safe rule.
- "Royalty-free" means a particular licence model, not copyright-free.
- Buying a sample library often permits use in a finished track, not resale of
  the isolated samples as a new Sample Pack.
- An uploader warranty is useful evidence/contractual protection, not proof of
  clearance or a complete defence for the platform.

### PRS/MCPS and PPL: obtain a service-specific answer

Describe the actual product to PRS for Music: user-uploaded repertoire,
on-demand full tracks, previews, permanent downloads, private grants, Sample
Packs, paid subscriptions, territories and expected usage. Obtain a written
assessment/quote for any represented composition/mechanical rights [L2].
Membership, a small audience, direct artist permission or a free tier does not
automatically resolve rights already controlled by publishers or societies.
Do not assume the smallest online licence covers this entire service.

PPL expressly identifies user-upload services and on-demand track streaming/
downloads among uses requiring direct recording-rightsholder licences rather
than its listed online licences [L3]. Its limited clip licence has conditions,
including no subscription/pay-per-play use; it is not a cheap substitute for
cre8's complete service. A venue's TheMusicLicence is not the answer either.

**Financial gate:** obtain any minima, usage royalties, reporting requirements
and repertoire/territory exclusions before paid or public music operation.
The hosting budgets contain **no quoted music royalties or collective fees**.
If rights cannot be established, restrict/remove that content rather than
launching it on the assumption that a later takedown will be enough.

## 3. Sample Pack buyer licences and rights complaints

Have the buyer licence state, in plain language:

- The seller/licensor identity, pack version, files and licence version.
- Permitted personal/commercial use in new productions and any limits.
- Whether attribution is required and whether rights are non-exclusive.
- Prohibitions on standalone redistribution, resale and sharing the source files.
- The position on Content ID claims, sample-library registration, machine-learning
  use and sublicensing, rather than leaving those rights ambiguous.
- Duration/territory, update policy, infringement reporting and remedies.

Preserve the accepted version, purchase record and immutable file manifest or
hashes. The current editable pack membership and free-form licence text are not
an adequate purchase ledger. A later pack edit must not silently replace what a
buyer bought. Preserve lawful purchase access when a seller cancels Pro, subject
to a documented takedown/refund process where continued delivery is unlawful.

Provide a visible rights-reporting route, an evidence-based review/removal
process, an appeal/escalation route and repeat-abuse handling. Prevent removed
content from continuing through CDN caches or share links. Preserve necessary
records without retaining unlawfully accessible files indefinitely.

Seek advice on the UK Electronic Commerce Regulations hosting-liability
conditions and cre8's actual role. Do not copy a US DMCA policy and assume that
it guarantees UK protection, or assume a notice-and-takedown process alone
licenses the service.

## 4. Consumer checkout, downloads and subscriptions

### Minimum customer-facing information

Before payment show the trader's name, geographic address/contact details,
product description/compatibility, full mandatory price, applicable taxes,
billing frequency, minimum commitment, renewal/cancellation terms and complaint
route. Let buyers correct order errors and use an unambiguous payment-obligation
button. Supply terms and confirmation on a durable medium, such as email [L4].
Keep evidence of the version accepted, not only a live editable web page.

Do not advertise a consumer price and add unavoidable processing/platform
charges at the end. Have any surcharge policy reviewed; do not pass through
prohibited consumer card surcharges.

### Digital downloads are not the same as Creator Pro

For immediately supplied paid digital content, the normal cancellation right
can be lost only where the required prior express consent to immediate supply
and acknowledgement of losing that right are obtained and confirmed [L4].
Record separate affirmative choices; do not rely on a pre-ticked box or a buried
terms clause. Have checkout wording legally reviewed.

Creator Pro may be a service or mixed contract, not simply a downloadable file.
Service commencement, proportionate payment and cancellation need the correct
rules; do not use a blanket download waiver to remove all subscription rights.
Loss of a change-of-mind right does **not** remove statutory remedies for faulty
or misdescribed content/services. Avoid blanket "no refunds" policies [L5].

### Subscription changes: confirm commencement before launch

At this review date, the October 2026 government material links **draft**
subscription guidance describing application to contracts from **1 January
2027**, with the underlying regulations subject to parliamentary approval [L6].
Do not treat this target as a verified final commencement date.

Have counsel check the enacted rules, transitional treatment and launch date.
Design now for clear pre-contract information, renewal/trial reminders,
straightforward online cancellation, cancellation confirmation and the required
initial/renewal cooling-off rights where applicable. Monthly billing is not an
automatic exemption. Keep a supported cancellation route if the portal fails.

## 5. Payments, payouts and fraud

For Pro, cre8 sells its own service. Use hosted checkout/billing, tokenized
provider identifiers and an accessible billing portal. Do not store card numbers
or CVVs. Hosted checkout reduces card-data exposure but does not automatically
remove every PCI, security, fraud or merchant obligation.
Support the provider's strong customer authentication/3-D Secure flows and
subsequent recurring-payment requirements rather than bypassing challenges.

A marketplace is different: identify who sells the pack, contracts with the
buyer, invoices, receives the funds, bears refunds/disputes and pays artists.
Distinguish a payment processor from a merchant of record; ordinary checkout
does not automatically make the provider responsible for every tax/refund duty.

The FCA warns that transferring funds between parties can be a regulated payment
service. The commercial-agent exclusion has conditions and does not simply cover
a platform acting for both buyer and seller [L7].

- Prefer a suitable regulated marketplace provider with connected seller
  accounts and its onboarding/KYC/payout processes.
- Obtain payment-perimeter advice on the exact arrangement; provider use is not
  by itself a legal exemption for cre8's activities.
- Do not create stored balances, escrow, a wallet or manual pooled artist
  payouts without specialist approval.
- Agree reserves, payout delays and negative-balance/refund recovery with the
  provider and sellers. A small accounting reserve is not legal safeguarding.
- Quote all products: Payments, Billing, Connect, Tax, currency conversion,
  payouts and disputes can have separate fees [L8].

For implementation: verify webhook signatures, reject replays, process events
idempotently, reconcile out-of-order/missing events and persist a server-side
entitlement ledger. A successful checkout redirect is not proof of payment.
Do not allow owner grants or A-List approval to bypass paid entitlements.

## 6. VAT, accounting and international exposure

HMRC currently publishes a UK VAT registration threshold of **GBP 90,000**:
test rolling 12-month taxable turnover and expected turnover in the next 30
days [L9]. Voluntary registration is possible. Reverse-charge overseas services
can affect registration turnover; obtain advice on Render/cloud/software bills.
Being below the threshold is not a general exemption from all tax obligations.

- If registered and standard-rating applies, a GBP 9 VAT-inclusive price contains
  GBP 1.50 output VAT; the business does not keep the full GBP 9.
- If not registered, do not charge VAT or issue documents implying it is.
- For digital marketplace sales, HMRC's rules can make the platform responsible
  for VAT on the underlying supply, not just its commission [L10].
  Have an accountant decide principal/agent/deemed-supplier treatment and
  turnover calculations from the actual contracts and checkout.
- Cross-border consumer digital sales may require destination VAT/GST compliance
  even when UK turnover is low. Check current non-Union OSS/local registration
  rules for EU sales; do not rely on UK thresholds or old MOSS terminology.
- Preserve appropriate customer-location evidence, tax records, receipts, refunds,
  provider settlements and seller statements. Agree retention schedules rather
  than deleting statutory records on account closure.
- Ask whether digital-platform seller reporting applies to the actual activities;
  digital files, personal services and events may need different analysis.
- Account for income/corporation tax and founder drawings/payroll separately.
  GMV is not necessarily platform revenue; cash receipts are not profit.

Start with UK-only selling if appropriate, but actually enforce it. Public
worldwide streams still raise international licensing/data issues even if only
UK residents can buy.

## 7. Privacy, online safety and the deployed stack

cre8 holds accounts, messages, media, access grants, analytics and push
subscriptions. Map controller/processor roles, lawful bases, privacy notices,
processor agreements, security, retention, access/deletion requests and incident
response. Check whether an ICO data-protection fee is due [L11].

Assess international transfers for Render, backups, Cloudflare and payment/
support providers, including subprocessors and remote access. A UK business,
an EU region or encryption alone does not settle every transfer question;
use applicable adequacy or appropriate safeguards where needed [L12].

Inventory cookies, local storage and analytics SDKs. Essential login/security
storage and optional tracking are different. Assess current PECR exemptions
and any applicable reform; obtain consent where required before optional
tracking. Marketing emails/push need a separate compliant basis and opt-out;
payment does not mean consent to all marketing [L13].

User uploads, discovery and messaging make an **Online Safety Act scope
assessment necessary**. If in scope, carry out the illegal-content risk
assessment, records, reporting/complaints and proportionate safety measures.
Complete the children's access assessment and, if applicable, the children's
risk assessment/protection measures [L14]. Small size or "18+" terms are not
automatic exclusions. Review the ICO Children's Code where the service is
likely to be accessed by under-18s [L15]. Decide age policy before launch, not
only at marketplace seller signup.

Operational controls must support the promises: secure admin access, grant
revocation, tested backups, rate limits, bounded ffmpeg jobs and lawful retention.
The optional PostgreSQL compatibility layer does not establish a commerce
ledger, production S3/R2 adapter, durable media pipeline or high availability.
Do not promise a statutory-rights waiver or uptime SLA to compensate for these gaps.

## 8. Legal/contract package and launch sign-off

| Deliverable | Review owner | Paid-launch gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entity, tax/VAT position and invoices | Founder + UK accountant | Before charging |
| User/creator terms and privacy/cookie notices | UK technology solicitor + product | Before inviting users to the relevant service |
| Music grants and any society/direct licences | Music solicitor + rightsholders | Before affected streaming/downloads, whether paid or free |
| Pro checkout, renewal, cancellation and refunds | Consumer-law review + product | Before subscriptions |
| Safety scope/risks, reporting and moderation | Founder/safety owner + adviser | Existing duties may already apply; do not wait for revenue |
| Buyer licence/version receipt and seller agreement | Music/commerce solicitor | Before paid packs |
| Payment money-flow and provider approval | Payment adviser + provider | Before seller funds flow |
| Tax/platform reporting determination | Accountant | Before marketplace launch |
| Third-party software, fonts, artwork and ffmpeg notices | Engineering + legal where needed | Before relevant distribution |

Audit actual dependency/build licences, especially ffmpeg codec/build choices:
do not infer LGPL/GPL/patent obligations just from an npm package name.
Provider approval and content policies also matter; unsupported business models
can lead to withheld funds or account closure.

These are not executable legal templates. Budget for tailored review and music
licensing quotes; the [business plan](./business-plan-uk.md) has provisional
allowances, not a guarantee of affordable clearance.
