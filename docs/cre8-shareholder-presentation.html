01 · The thesis

Make the release workflow the reason to return.

Creators share unreleased music across chats, drives and promo tools. cre8 brings permissioned sharing and scene discovery into one focused place.

“Share unreleased music with the people you choose. Keep your dubs, packs and DJ community organized in one trusted place.”

Positioning direction to test — not approved marketing copy or a claim that the service is leak-proof.

Workflow

Less scattered release admin

Organize packs, sharing and recipients around the music itself.

Trust

Creator-controlled access

Keep permission and revocation distinct from paid promotion or fan paywalls.

Community

Context creates repeat value

Discovery, crates and relationships give the workflow a reason to recur.

Hypothesis to validate with active creators and listeners.02 / 12

A working product foundation; commerce is a separate build.

Existing product surfaces

03 · Positioning

The whitespace is a combination, not a feature moat.

Free substitutes

Chat + cloud links

Discord, messaging, Dropbox and WeTransfer are familiar and often “good enough” for basic sharing.

Threat: creators may not feel enough pain to switch.

Adjacent platforms

Creator & promo tools

SoundCloud, sample subscriptions and label-facing promo products bundle reach, production or campaign features.

Threat: incumbents can add adjacent workflows quickly.

cre8 hypothesis

Niche release relationship

Combine trusted community, pack organization and explicitly granted pre-release listening.

Test: does this save repeated effort and bring people back?

Avoid competing as a smaller SoundCloud. The sharper bet is scene-level trust around creator-controlled access.

Desk research is not a complete competitor census. Public prices are snapshots and are not directly comparable.[1]

Possible position; not demonstrated differentiation or defensibility.04 / 12

08 · Go-to-market pilot

Prove repeat behavior with a small, relevant cohort.

1

Listen

Interview 10 active creators and 5 DJs/listeners about their last real release-share workflow.

2

Recruit

Find 5–10 anchor creators with cleared content and relevant collaborators.

3

Charge honestly

Ask for paid commitments at the shown price; launch only a delivered Pro workflow.

4

Observe renewals

Track repeat use, renewals, support hours, contribution and access incidents.

Proposed decision gate · not achieved yet

Five credible paid pilot commitments

Then 5–10 paying pilot creators and at least 4 of the first 5 renewing after two billing cycles.

Measure the right denominator

Eligible active creators, not registrations

A 100-user waitlist is not product-market fit. Track activation, repeat use, paid conversion and renewal separately.

Small-pilot gates are learning thresholds, not statistical proof.09 / 12

09 · Principal risks

Trust and rights are launch conditions, not footnotes.

RiskWhy it mattersControl / gate
Rights & licensingUploaded or sold recordings may include uncleared masters, compositions, samples or performances.Rights audit and specialist quote; keep affected content out until cleared.
Free substitutes & retentionChat and cloud links may be sufficient; niche communities can struggle to sustain repeat activity.Measure recurring workflow pain, return use and renewals; stop or revise if weak.
Payments & consumer dutiesSubscriptions, refunds, seller payouts and marketplace tax add legal and operational complexity.Defer paid packs; approve terms, billing and payout flows before accepting money.
Infrastructure & supportMedia delivery, backups and founder support may exceed the small-launch allowance.Measure media and service costs; cap usage; test restores; reforecast before growth.
Competitive responseLarge creator platforms can extend distribution, access and monetization features.Compete on focused scene relationships and workflow quality; revisit landscape often.
